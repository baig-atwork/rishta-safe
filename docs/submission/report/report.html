Rishta Safe

Technical report

An education and decision-support companion for the UAE premarital genetic-screening pathway: it explains a reported result in plain English, Arabic and Urdu, shows who may see what, and hands complex cases to a human by fixed rule. Synthetic data only.

11 scoreboard measures: 9 run on synthetic data (solid), 2 not run (hatched).
Status in one box
Prototype on made-up data
Every person, result and timeline is synthetic. It is not a medical device, a diagnosis or clinical advice.
Not clinically reviewed
Every clinical rule, cut-off and detection rate awaits sign-off from the clinical lead (sign-off pending). Arabic and Urdu text is machine-drafted with no native clinical review. No comprehension study, field validation or real data stands behind it.
No hosting claim
Nothing is deployed. The demo is shown running locally. Every outside system is simulated and labelled.
On the name. “Safe” in Rishta Safe is not a safety or compatibility verdict on a couple. No screen, label or score tells anyone whether to marry, and the three result labels are not a severity scale.

THE CASE IN ONE PAGEA narrow tool, with its weakest number first

The problem

Screening helps only if people understand a carrier or couple-level result, reach qualified counselling and choose in line with their own values, without one family member’s information reaching another unasked. The organisers supply no data, so everything here is built and measured on made-up couples.

What it does

  • Explains a result a licensed programme has already reported, in three languages, from reviewed templates.
  • Shows who may see what under each emirate’s published rule.
  • Escalates to a human by 21 fixed rules.
  • Follows the real pathway (15 states) and closes the loop with a clinician proforma.

What it does not do

  • Screen, test, diagnose, certify or counsel.
  • Give a marriage decision, a score or a residual-risk number.
  • Run a language model at run time.
  • Connect to any real system, or claim to be hosted.
Read the weakest number first. The refusal check refused 36 of 59 held-out prompts on its first run (61%). The rows at 100% are a regression gate, not safety evidence: the same team wrote both sides of every comparison. Two of eleven measures were not run, so no comprehension or clinical-validity claim is made.

  • The entry is deliberately narrow: it explains a result a licensed programme has already reported, shows who may see what, escalates by rule, tracks the pathway and closes the loop. It does not screen, test or counsel.
  • Views exist for a couple member, a counsellor, a clinician, a programme operator (aggregates only) and a read-only evaluator. Every result screen states that it is education and decision support, never clinical advice.
  • It does not hide data from the state, which is a statutory recipient (Federal Decree-Law 49/2023 Art. 26(2)), and it is not connected to Emirates Health Services, Malaffi, the Emirati Genome Programme, DoH or DHA: every such connection is simulated and labelled.
  • We do not claim to be first at laboratory-value interpretation. We claim a traceable rule trace, and a record of what each number does not show.

2 / ARCHITECTUREPure-domain packages behind one composition layer

  • One caller across packages. Genetics computes and never decides who sees what; governance decides and never words anything; explain words and never computes. Only the composition layer calls across them.
  • Small and typed. Backend on Python 3.13 (FastAPI, Pydantic v2, structlog, uv) with eight runtime dependencies, none a model client. 44 operations on 40 paths under /v1 plus three health routes; errors are RFC 9457 problem details with a correlation id.
  • State in memory behind small repository classes, so a database adapter can replace them (parked). The demo is seeded, resettable and single-replica; the clock is injected, so every timing is simulated.
  • No text is generated at run time. A numeric hallucination in a genetic result is the worst failure available; the best verifier we found agreed with clinicians only 89 to 93 percent of the time; Arabic and Urdu model output cannot be measured honestly without native reviewers. The cost is less fluent text and slower growth of wording. A cloud language model may have helped draft static template text and AI coding assistants helped write code and documents: both are declared, no personal or realistic data went to either, and the provider’s output terms were not reviewed.

3 / THE RESULTOne result, end to end: three closed labels, no scale

  • Natural frequencies, fixed denominator. Two carriers of one recessive condition: 25 affected, 50 carrier and 25 neither per 100 pregnancies, as integers; X-linked conditions give lines per 100 sons and per 100 daughters. Carriers of different conditions at the haemoglobin beta locus pair into a compound disorder.
  • Never counted as a result: a variant of uncertain significance. A pending report or a blood-count-only work-up can never give a shared or no-shared statement, and a locus is compared only when both panels cover it.
  • Staff see more, couples do not. A review priority (routine, priority, urgent) is for staff only; the severity tiers and priorities are our own illustrative rules awaiting sign-off.
  • Why the vocabulary is closed: the programme’s own words can read as a verdict on a couple. Banned-word lists in three languages (38, 38 and 32 terms plus match patterns) are enforced by the checker.

  • Structure and times follow the Department of Health Abu Dhabi Premarital Screening Standard V3 (September 2024) and the Dubai Health Authority Standards for Premarital Screening Services V1 (effective 27 March 2026). We wrote original wording citing the clause followed and did not copy forms, layouts, risk statements or the gene list.
  • The screening visit is a simulated hand-off to a licensed centre; neither standard has home collection. A partner who declines to share leads to certificate_withheld, the tension both standards describe, shown rather than hidden.
  • Seven outside systems are simulated and labelled in all three languages. Referral tasks are shaped like an HL7 FHIR R5 Task; no FHIR server exists. Four invariants are property-tested over random event sequences: no flagged couple without a counselling task; a rejected or failed task spawns a replacement; no case closes with an open task or, if flagged, without a clinician proforma; times never go backwards.

  • Consent ledger. Append-only: a grant is one line, a revocation another. Scopes are couple_result_only, gene_names_only and full_variant_detail, none by default. Mandatory couple screening is not consent-based (Art. 4(2)(a), 15); it is recorded with a statutory basis that grants nothing and cannot be revoked. Revocation stops disclosure and deletes nothing: the Art. 8 deletion workflow is cut.
  • Right not to know (Art. 9(2)) removes everything result-related from that person’s view. Where life may be threatened the statute compels disclosure but does not say an app must deliver it, so the app view stays withheld and an accredited professional is alerted, flagged for a legal read.
  • Audit chain. Each event stores the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON and the previous hash, with pseudonymous ids and no free text. Tests tamper with, reorder, delete from and insert into it. It cannot detect truncation of its tail without an external anchor, which is not built, and it is held in memory.
  • Third parties. An employer, insurer or relative request is always refused with HTTP 403 citing Art. 19 to 21, and logged.

  • Refused: diagnosis, treatment advice, “should we marry”, termination advice, lineage inference, employer and insurer questions, and another person’s result; marriage, termination, lineage and other-person requests also notify a counsellor. The classifier is keyword and pattern matching, not language understanding.

12 / THE PRODUCTION PATHA shadow pilot after five gates, and what engineering remains

  • Gates before anyone sees it: an ethics determination by the DoH research ethics committee; the clinical lead’s sign-off; native clinical review of Arabic and Urdu; the outstanding legal reads against Decree-Law 49/2023; the facility’s ADHICS and Responsible AI risk assessment; and an independent privacy audit.
  • Pilot measures: the 90 percent referral target, time to counsellor brief, teach-back comprehension with intervals, and escalation recall against a clinician-labelled set. Cancelled marriages are never a measure.
  • Engineering remaining: an identity provider (UAE PASS or Emirates ID for couples, the facility’s sign-on for staff); a Postgres adapter with reversible migrations, row-level security and append-only audit storage with an anchored head hash; monitoring beyond JSON logs.
  • Hosting. Nothing is hosted. Federal Law 2/2019 Art. 13 and the DoH Responsible AI Standard cl. 3.1.4.5.1 (the first read from secondary summaries) point to keeping identifiable data and compute within the country, and no run-time model call exists that could send data abroad. The demo is shown running locally.
  • Commercial path: a hypothesis, with no buyer contacted, a labelled pricing assumption and no revenue figure.

The scoreboard’s own caveats, verbatim

  • All people, results and timelines are synthetic. Nothing here measures a real programme.
  • Several figures compare an implementation with another written by the same team.
  • Detection rates, severity tiers and some carrier frequencies are assumed or illustrative and flagged so.
  • Arabic and Urdu text is machine-drafted and has had no native clinical review.
  • No clinical review, field validation or comprehension study has been done.
M8 (human comprehension) was not run: a protocol exists and the ethics exemption question is unanswered. At n=30 an 80% score has a 95% interval of 61 to 92%, so the study would be reported with intervals, never as proof. M9 (clinical review of rules, detection rates and translations) was not done: sign-off by the clinical lead for rules and a native clinical reviewer for the languages is pending.
