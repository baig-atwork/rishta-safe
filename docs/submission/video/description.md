# Rishta Safe: demo video description

**Title:** Rishta Safe: a couple-result companion for premarital genetic screening (prototype, synthetic data)

**Short description:** Rishta Safe is a prototype that explains a premarital genetic screening result in plain words, in each partner's language side by side, and lets each person decide who sees what. This video walks through it in under three minutes. It is an entry for the Abu Dhabi Future Health Digital Innovation Challenge, Challenge 03: Understanding and Reducing Inherited Disease Risk.

Everything shown uses synthetic data. Rishta Safe is education and decision support. It is not a medical device and it is not clinical advice.

## What you will see

- A result that is not shown until the person chooses how to meet it: now, with a counsellor first, not yet, or not at all. Choosing not to know is recorded as a legal right (Federal Decree-Law 49 of 2023, Article 9(2)).
- The same result in English and Arabic side by side, with one hundred pregnancies as a grid. The headline is one of three categories (shared result found, no shared change found, more information needed). The categories are not a severity scale.
- A who-sees-what matrix in which only the first column is the person's to change, and an audit row for every change.
- A staff instrument with a queue, a one-page brief and a trace that links each line to its rule and source. Nothing is generated and it recommends nothing.
- An Evidence page with live probes (an employer asks for a result; a question about whether to go ahead with a wedding) and a scoreboard that shows its weakest figure first and marks two measures as not run.

## What this is not

- Not clinically reviewed. The clinical rules are pending sign-off.
- No real patient data. Every person, result and timeline is synthetic.
- No comprehension study has been run.
- The Arabic and Urdu text are machine drafts awaiting native clinical review.
- No connection to any UAE health system.

The narration is a synthetic voice reading the script, and the captions are burned in. A caption file (SRT) and the narration text accompany the video.
