# ADR 0203: Regulatory posture: assert the intended use, do not claim an exemption

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Deciders:** the declarations lane (G1), under the architecture contract (rules 9, 10 and 14)

## Context
No UAE text we read says that calling software education or decision support changes whether it is a medical device, and intended-purpose regimes generally look at what software does (R01 section 2.6, secondary sources). Federal Decree-Law 38/2024 created the Emirates Drug Establishment, and no guidance specific to AI or software as a medical device was found (Context note). The Decree-Law readings in the dossier carry audit corrections: Art. 4(1) and 6 are the rule for voluntary screening, Art. 4(2)(a) exempts obligatory screening, and Art. 8(1) is limited to voluntary screening. Art. 25(3) restricts publishing programme statistics. Federal Law 2/2019 and Ministerial Resolution 51/2021 were read from secondary summaries only.

## Decision
The regulatory posture page states Rishta Safe's intended use and what it does not do, and says plainly that a label does not decide device status. It uses only the dossier's article readings with their audit corrections, never describes the mandatory couple screening as consented, and states that legal reads are outstanding. It contains no programme statistic. It records the localisation default and the DoH Responsible AI clause as design targets, and never claims in-UAE hosting for the demo.

## Consequences
We make a narrower and more defensible claim than "not a device". A pilot must settle classification and obtain legal reads before any commercial claim.

## Revisit trigger
A UAE guidance document on software as a medical device, or a legal read of the outstanding Decree-Law items.
